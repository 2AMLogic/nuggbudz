**Dim 3 = 2/5.** The deck declines to size the market: no TAM, no SAM, no SOM,
no comparable transactions. That is a deliberate, documented decision
(`BRIEF.md` §Market, `outline.md` §Cuts) and it is the *right* decision for this
audience — an invented top-down number would have been a critical flag. But this
dimension scores sizing credibility, and there is no sizing to score. The two
points awarded are for what is there instead: a per-pairing unit that is
arithmetically checked, labelled as arithmetic, and honest about what it does
not know. No perspective sibling exists, so no substrate uplift is available.

**Dim 4 = 4/5.** Differentiation is mechanism-level rather than adjectival: the
cell is the unit of matching, the settlement is exact so no negotiation is
needed between strangers, and the queue rule makes the orderer role fall out of
the data. One point withheld because no moat language is on the slide — why a
chain app or a delivery app could not simply add this is argued in the speaker
notes and nowhere on the deck.

**Arithmetic re-check (independent)**: two solo boxes at $6.99 = $13.98; less
the $7.99 bulk box = $5.99 spread; $5.99/$13.98 = 42.8% -> 43%. $7.99 + $0.99 =
$8.98 collected; halved = $4.49 each; $6.99 - $4.49 = $2.50 saved, 35.8% -> 36%.
$0.99/$8.98 = 11.0%. Fee x 10/25/100 = $9.90/$24.75/$99.00; party savings $5.00
x the same = $50.00/$125.00/$500.00. Every figure on the catalogue slide
reproduces from `shared/deals.ts`. **No market-math error.**

No critical flag: no top-down sizing is presented, so the "top-down-only"
disqualifier does not fire, and the arithmetic that *is* present checks out.
