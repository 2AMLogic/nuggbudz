# Market findings — `nuggbudz-hackathon.1`

## major — no market sizing at all (accepted, but it costs dim 3)

The deck cannot reach the top of dim 3 without a bottom-up sizing case, and this
one has no sizing case by design. The operator should know the trade explicitly:
against the fundraising rubric this caps the achievable total at roughly 46/49
even if every other dimension were perfect.

**Recommendation**: do NOT invent a TAM to close the gap. If this deck is ever
reused for a raise, the honest path is a bottom-up build (stores in a metro x
cell-hours x pairings per cell-hour), and the pilot ask on slide 13 exists
precisely to measure the missing input. Keep the current framing for the
hackathon.

## minor — two of three competitor rows name categories, not products

`BRIEF.md` attests DoorDash and Uber Eats by name; the slide says "Delivery
group orders". Named competitors are more credible than categories and the
brief already permits the names. "Chain apps" similarly could name the three
chains that are already on the catalogue slide.

**Fix**: name them on the slide. No new attestation needed.

## minor — no moat clause on the differentiation slide

The mechanism is strong but the "why can't a chain app add this" answer lives
only in the speaker notes. One clause on the slide would convert an implicit
argument into a scored one.

**Fix**: add a single line to the competition slide — matching strangers is a
cross-merchant, cross-cell liquidity problem, which is exactly the thing a
single chain's app is structurally worst at.
