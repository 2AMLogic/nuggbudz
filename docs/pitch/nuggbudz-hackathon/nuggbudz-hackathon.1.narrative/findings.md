# Narrative findings — `nuggbudz-hackathon.1`

## major — slide 13: three asks in one line, in the wrong order

"One pilot cell for 30 days · Stripe Connect with the fee as an application fee
· first settled paid split" reads as one undifferentiated string. Two of the
three are not asks at all — the payments rail is a commitment and the settled
split is the milestone the pilot buys. The ask a judge can act on *today* is
buried in the speaker notes.

**Fix**: put the judge-facing ask in the headline, then one line that separates
what we are asking for (a pilot cell) from what it buys (the first settled paid
split). Keep it to two lines; the ask slide's capacity is tighter than a content
slide.

## minor — slide 10 heading stalls the arc

Every other heading carries a claim; "It is shipped" carries a topic. Coming
directly before the demo, this is the worst place in the deck for the energy to
drop.

**Fix**: assert the claim — deployed *and* verified against production.

## minor — the bridge from slide 8 to slide 9 is implicit

Slide 8 closes the money argument; slide 9 opens the systems argument. A judge
tracking "why should I believe you can find the second buyer" has to make that
jump unaided. One clause in the slide-9 supporting line already does most of
the work; the speaker notes should carry the rest explicitly.

## nit — the title tagline and the problem headline both open on price

Not a defect: the repetition is deliberate reinforcement. Noted so the reviser
does not "fix" it into vagueness.

## Positives

- The outline's beat order is honoured exactly; no topic-bucket drift.
- Cut slides (team, financials, market sizing) are documented with rationale
  rather than silently dropped — the arc does not have holes where they were.
- The appendix earns its place: it answers the one question this audience will
  actually ask ("are these numbers real?").
